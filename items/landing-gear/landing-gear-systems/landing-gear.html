Section 1: Landing Gear Systems

The landing gear is the set of legs and wheels an aircraft stands on. It carries the aircraft's weight on the ground, soaks up the bump of landing, and folds away in flight to cut air resistance (drag). Most aircraft have a nose gear at the front and two main gears under the wings or body.

Where is the landing gear?

A fighter jet has a tricycle layout, like a child's tricycle: one nose gear under the front of the body, just behind the cockpit, and two main gears further back, one on each side, near the wings. The main gears sit close to the aircraft's balance point, so they carry most of its weight. The nose gear carries less weight and steers the aircraft on the ground.

Section 2: Hydraulic System Visualisation

Landing gear is heavy, so most aircraft move it with hydraulics: a special oil, called hydraulic fluid, pushed hard through pipes. You can't squash a liquid, so when you push it at one end, it pushes just as hard at the other end. That push is strong enough to swing a whole gear leg.

Press GEAR DOWN or GEAR UP and follow the fluid, one stage at a time.

Pressure: fluid going to work Return: fluid going back No flow

All three gears are UP (the aircraft is flying). Selector valve: NEUTRAL.

Inside each actuating cylinder: blue is fluid flowing in and pushing the piston; red is fluid being pushed out. The rod is joined to the gear leg, so as the piston moves, the leg swings down into the airflow or up into its bay.

    Section 3: Landing Gear Indication

    The pilot can't see the wheels from the cockpit, so lights show where the gear is. A sensor on each leg turns its green light on only when that leg is locked down. The golden rule: three greens = safe to land.

    Move the gear handle (or use the buttons). Watch the lights in the cockpit and the real gear on the aircraft.

    What the lights are telling you

    UP AND LOCKED

    All lights off with the handle UP: the gear is tucked away and locked. Normal for flight.

    What the lights mean

    Lights you seeWhat it meansWhat you should doIf ignored
    Three green, red off All three legs are down and locked. Check and say out loud "three greens". Safe to land. If you don't check, you could miss a leg that never locked.
    Red on, greens off Gear is in transit: moving between up and down. Doors are open. Wait for the sequence to finish (usually a few seconds). Don't move the handle back and forth. Landing now could collapse the gear. Open doors also add drag and noise.
    Red stays on, fewer than three greens Unsafe: at least one leg is not locked, or it doesn't match the handle. Do not land. Follow the checklist: lamp test, try the gear again, use the emergency (backup) extension. Report it. A leg can fold on touchdown: serious damage to the aircraft and risk of injury.
    All off, handle UP Gear is up and locked, doors closed. Normal in flight. Nothing during cruise. Before landing, select GEAR DOWN and wait for three greens. Forgetting to lower the gear leads to a wheels-up (belly) landing.
    Red off, but fewer than three greens, handle DOWN Something is wrong with the lights: often a blown bulb or a power fault. The gear may be fine, but you can't be sure. Treat it as unsafe. Press lamp test to check the bulbs, check the circuit breaker, and report it. You might land with gear that is not locked.

    Summary

    Key takeaways

    1. The landing gear carries the aircraft on the ground, absorbs landing shock and retracts in flight to reduce drag.
    2. The oleo strut is the shock absorber: oil and gas inside cushion the landing.
    3. Drag braces hold the leg against front-to-back forces; side braces hold it against sideways forces. Both lock straight when the gear is down.
    4. Torque links let the strut slide up and down but stop the wheels twisting out of line.
    5. Hydraulic fluid flows from the reservoir to the pump, which pressurises it. The selector valve sends it to the correct side of the actuating cylinder.
    6. Fluid pushed out of the other side of the cylinder returns to the reservoir through the return line. The system is a closed loop.
    7. Three greens = down and locked. Red = in transit or unsafe. All off with the handle up = up and locked.
    8. The pump, filter, check valve and relief valve keep the fluid pushing hard, clean, one-way and at a safe pressure.
    9. Never ignore a red light or a missing green. Do a lamp test, try the gear again, and if it still won't lock, use the emergency extension, which lets the gear fall by its own weight. Report every fault.
