Pitot-Static Flight Instruments

How air pressure becomes the numbers on the cockpit instruments. This is a generic training cockpit, not a real aircraft.

    1. System Overview

    An aircraft cannot feel how fast or how high it is. Instead, it measures air pressure and turns it into readings on three cockpit instruments.

    Three pressures to know

    Total pressure

    The pitot tube faces the airflow. Air is pushed into it, so it senses total pressure (also called pitot pressure).

    Static pressure

    A static port is a small hole on the side of the aircraft. Air slides past it, so it senses the static pressure of the air around the aircraft.

    Dynamic pressure

    Dynamic = Total − Static

    This is the extra push caused by moving through the air. Faster flight gives a bigger difference.

    Three instruments use them

    InstrumentWhat it usesWhat it shows
    Airspeed indicatorPitot (total) pressure and static pressureSpeed, from the difference between the two
    AltimeterStatic pressureHeight. Static pressure falls as height increases.
    Vertical speed indicatorChanges in static pressure over timeHow fast the aircraft climbs or descends

    Key points

    • Pitot tube: total pressure. Static ports: static pressure.
    • Dynamic pressure is total pressure minus static pressure.
    • Airspeed indicator: pitot and static. Altimeter: static. Vertical speed indicator: static pressure changing over time.
