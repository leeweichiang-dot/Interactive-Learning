Pitot-Static Flight Instruments

How air pressure becomes the numbers on the cockpit instruments. This is a generic training cockpit, not a real aircraft. All values are simulated.

    Glossary of key terms

    Words with a dotted underline show a short meaning. Hover over them, move to them with the Tab key, or tap them.

    Pitot tube
    A small tube at the front of the aircraft. It faces the oncoming air and senses total pressure.
    Static port
    A small hole on the side of the aircraft. Air slides past it, so it senses static pressure.
    Total pressure
    The pressure measured at the open front of the pitot tube. Moving air is pushed in, so it is higher than static pressure. It is also called pitot pressure.
    Static pressure
    The pressure of the air around the aircraft. It gets lower as the aircraft goes higher.
    Dynamic pressure
    The extra push from moving through the air. Dynamic pressure = total pressure − static pressure. It gets bigger as the aircraft goes faster.
    Airspeed indicator
    A cockpit dial that shows how fast the aircraft moves through the air. It compares total pressure with static pressure.
    Altimeter
    A cockpit dial that shows height. It reads static pressure.
    Vertical speed indicator
    A cockpit dial that shows how fast the aircraft goes up or down. It reads how quickly static pressure is changing.
    Climb
    To go up to a greater height. In a climb, static pressure falls.
    Descent
    Going down to a lower height. In a descent, static pressure rises.
    Blockage
    Something that stops air or pressure from getting through, such as ice, dirt or water. “Blocked” means there is a blockage.

    1. System Overview

    An aircraft cannot feel how fast or how high it is. Instead, it measures air pressure. It turns the pressure into readings on three cockpit instruments (dials in front of the pilot).

    Three pressures to know

    Total pressure

    The pitot tube is a small tube at the front of the aircraft. It faces the air. Air is pushed into it, so it senses total pressure (also called pitot pressure).

    Static pressure

    A static port is a small hole on the side of the aircraft. Air slides past it. It senses static pressure: the pressure of the air around the aircraft.

    Dynamic pressure

    Dynamic = Total − Static

    This is the extra push from moving through the air. Faster flight gives a bigger difference.

    Three instruments use them

    Each instrument is a dial in the cockpit.

    InstrumentWhat it usesWhat it shows
    Airspeed indicatorPitot (total) pressure and static pressureSpeed, from the difference between the two
    AltimeterStatic pressureHeight. Static pressure falls as height increases.
    Vertical speed indicatorChanges in static pressure over timeHow fast the aircraft goes up (climbs) or down (descends)

    Key points

    • Pitot tube: total pressure. Static ports: static pressure.
    • Dynamic pressure is total pressure minus static pressure.
    • Airspeed indicator: pitot and static. Altimeter: static. Vertical speed indicator: static pressure changing over time.
